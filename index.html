📖 Methodology

How the NCAA Scouting Dashboard calculates player value, fit scores, and team analysis.

📡 Data Sources

MBB — Stats come from the College Basketball Data (CBD) API via a Cloudflare Worker proxy. Includes advanced metrics: BPM, WS/40, DRtg, ORtg, USG%, eFG%, TS%, A/TO, OR%. Player class is inferred from multi-season career history (2022–2026). Draft probability is available for MBB only.

WBB — Stats come directly from the ESPN WBB byathlete API (CORS-friendly, fetched in-browser). Conference mapping is sourced from the ESPN groups endpoint. Player height is enriched in the background from ESPN team rosters (~10s after page load).

WBB available stats: PPG, RPG, APG, SPG, BPG, FG%, 3P%, FT%, TOPG, eFG%*, TS%*, A/TO*, USG%†

WBB removed stats (not in ESPN API): BPM, WS/40, DRtg, ORtg, OR%, DR% — these are dropped from WBB scoring weights entirely rather than fabricated.

* eFG%, TS%, A/TO are derived from raw counting stats. † USG% is approximated as (FGA + 0.44×FTA + TOV) / 80.7 × 100, where 80.7 is the estimated average possession-actions per game in NCAA WBB.

🎯 PerfScore

PerfScore is a weighted composite of key statistical percentiles. Each stat is normalized to a 0–1 scale between your configured Min and Max, then multiplied by its weight. Higher-is-better stats (like PPG, APG) produce higher percentiles at the top; lower-is-better stats (like DRtg, TOPG) are automatically inverted.

Formula: PerfScore = Σ(weight × normalized_stat) / Σ(weight)

Percentiles are computed within the selected position group (Guards or Bigs) and league (MBB or WBB). Players are only compared to peers in the same group.

💰 Valuation

Player valuation uses an exponential curve anchored to your average pay setting. The curve is calibrated so the average player earns avgPay and a star-level player earns starValue:

k = ln(starValue / avgPay) / (starPerf − avgPerf)
Predicted = clamp(minPay, maxPay, avgPay × exp(k × (PerfScore − avgPerf)))

A minutes multiplier (min(1, sqrt(MP / MP_p))) is then applied to reduce inflation for low-minutes players. If the Conference Multiplier is enabled, PerfScore is boosted/reduced by the conference strength factor before valuation — MAC = 1.00 is the baseline.

📊 Position Groups

Players are bucketed into two position groups for all percentile calculations:

  • Guards — Positions: G, G-F
  • Bigs — Positions: F, C, F-C, C-F

Guards are never compared to Bigs, and vice versa. Switch between groups using the Guards / Bigs tab in the Players page. The Team Builder analyzes gaps for whichever position groups exist on your roster.

🏷️ Role Tags

Role tags are archetype labels assigned based on percentile thresholds across key stats. A player can hold multiple tags simultaneously. Examples:

  • Elite Scorer — PPG ≥ 80th percentile
  • Floor General — APG ≥ 75th, A/TO ≥ 70th
  • 3-and-D — 3P% ≥ 65th, SPG ≥ 60th
  • Rim Anchor — BPG ≥ 70th, RPG ≥ 65th (Bigs)
  • Stretch Big — 3P% ≥ 60th (Bigs)
  • High Usage — USG% ≥ 75th

Tags appear in player profiles and drive the similar-player matching algorithm.

🏆 Team Builder

The Team Builder lets you assemble a roster within budget and slot constraints:

  • Budget — Total NIL budget across all players
  • Player cap — Maximum per-player valuation
  • Max roster — Maximum roster size
  • Guards / Bigs targets — Position slot targets for balance
  • Weak below — Percentile threshold for flagging swap candidates

The Team Stat Profile shows your roster's average percentile across key categories. Red (weak) areas signal recruiting gaps. The Recommended Fits panel surfaces players who address your weakest categories within budget.

Use the ⚔️ Head-to-Head sub-tab to compare your team vs. an opponent stat-by-stat, and the 🎯 Opponent sub-tab to build and scout your next opponent.

🏀 WBB Scoring Defaults

WBB uses separate default scoring weights calibrated to WBB stat distributions (which differ significantly from MBB — e.g. WBB average PPG ~12 vs MBB ~14, fewer 3-point attempts, higher turnover rates).

Guards (default weights): PPG 14 · eFG% 13 · A/TO 11 · SPG 11 · TOPG 11 · APG 13 · 3P% 9 · FT% 6 · RPG 6 · USG% 6

Bigs (default weights): RPG 20 · BPG 16 · eFG% 14 · PPG 12 · SPG 8 · A/TO 8 · TOPG 8 · USG% 8 · FT% 6

Min/max ranges for each stat are also calibrated to WBB distributions (e.g. PPG Guard max is 25, not 30 as in MBB). Conference multipliers use the same MAC=1.00 baseline but map to WBB conferences. Draft Radar grades are MBB only — WBB draft dynamics differ significantly and no WBB draft model is included.

🎲 Monte Carlo Match Simulation

The Teams Hub Monte Carlo sim projects game outcomes by running thousands of score simulations from each team's adjusted efficiency ratings and pace.

  • Regulation model — Expected scoring is computed from offense-vs-defense blends (AdjO/AdjD) and average pace; each team gets game-to-game scoring noise (SD controls) with optional score correlation (ρ).
  • OT-aware logic — Regulation ties now trigger overtime simulation instead of ending as ties. OT uses a 5-minute pace-scaled scoring model with lower OT volatility and repeats until a winner (capped periods + rare fallback tiebreak).
  • Core outputs — Win%, projected score, average/median margin, close-game rate, blowout rates, and margin distribution histogram.
  • OT outputs — Overtime rate, regulation-tie rate, and overtime winner split by team for late-game prep context.
  • Scope — This is a probabilistic scenario engine (not a possession-level play-caller). Use with shot chart, matchup, and scouting context for full prep.
🏀 Draft Radar

The Draft Radar predicts NBA draft probability from college stats using a sign-constrained elastic-net logistic regression model with balanced class weights, trained on 2,391 NCAA players from the 2019–2025 drafts (326 drafted, 2,065 undrafted), sourced from the College Basketball Data API.

Model Architecture

Elastic-net logistic regression with 10 features: 7 statistical z-scores + 3 categorical variables. Regularization uses both L1 (λ=0.005) and L2 (λ=0.03) penalties. All stat weights constrained ≥ 0 via projected gradient descent. Balanced class weights in the loss function (drafted samples weighted 3.67×) correct the 6:1 class imbalance and produce well-calibrated probabilities.

Statistical Features (z-scored against dataset means)

  • PPG — Points per game (μ=14.8, σ=3.5) — regularized to ~0: scoring volume doesn't discriminate drafted vs. undrafted
  • WS/40 — Win Shares per 40 min (μ=0.13, σ=0.06) — strongest stat predictor
  • eFG% — Effective FG% (μ=51.2%, σ=5.3%)
  • BPG — Blocks per game (μ=0.38, σ=0.46) — rim protection highly valued
  • RPG — Rebounds per game (μ=4.8, σ=1.9)
  • APG, SPG — Assists and steals per game (regularized near zero)

BPM and USG% were dropped to eliminate multicollinearity (BPM↔WS/40 r=0.80, PPG↔USG% r=0.87, VIF up to 14.4). Their signals are captured by the remaining features.

Categorical Features

  • Conference tier — Power=1, Mid-major=0, Low-major=−1 (highest importance, weight=1.29)
  • Class — Youth premium: Fr=3, So=2, Jr=1, Sr/Grad=0 (weight=0.52). Inferred at runtime from multi-season CBD API data (2022–2026): a player's first appearance determines their class year. CBD API does not provide class directly.
  • Starter flag — 1 if MPG > 25, else 0 (weight=−0.22)

Why No Interaction Terms?

Earlier model versions used 6 interaction terms (PPG×USG%, BPM×Class, etc.). These caused suppressor variable artifacts: multiplying two negative z-scores produced false positives (bench players rated A+). The interaction model's 6% CV F1 advantage did not offset the catastrophic out-of-distribution failures. Removing interactions trades in-sample fit for robust generalization.

Validation

5-fold cross-validation: 86.7% accuracy, 50.9% precision, 90.5% recall, 64.7% F1. Final model on all data: 86.8% accuracy, 50.9% precision, 90.2% recall, 65.0% F1, TP=294 FP=284 FN=32 TN=1781. The balanced-weight approach prioritizes recall (catching actual draft picks) over raw precision. 90% of drafted players are correctly identified.

Feature Importance (|weight × std|)

Ranked: Conference (1.06) > WS/40 (0.77) > Class (0.53) > BPG (0.43) > RPG (0.16) > eFG% (0.14)

Additional Features

  • Quantified Development Path — Identifies stats below drafted-player averages with actionable advice. Each recommendation shows the exact probability impact of reaching the target: e.g., "Improving eFG% from 45.0% → 51.2% shifts draft probability by +X%"
  • Draft Comparables — Finds the 3 most statistically similar drafted players (from a 119-player pool of lottery + notable picks) using weighted Euclidean distance on z-scored features.
  • Scouting Insights — Readable takeaways about scoring efficiency, defensive impact, youth premium, and competition level

Data Pipeline

  • Source — College Basketball Data API via Cloudflare Worker proxy
  • Seasons — Player stats for 2018–2025; draft picks for 2019–2025
  • Class inference — Multi-season appearance tracking (first appearance = Fr, +1 year per subsequent season). Used both in training (build-draft-dataset.js) and at runtime (data.js loadCareerSeasons) since the CBD API does not provide class/eligibility data.
  • Undrafted pool — Players with PPG ≥ 5, MPG ≥ 20, G ≥ 15 who were not drafted, selected by total minutes played (~6:1 undrafted:drafted ratio)
  • Build script — tools/build-draft-dataset.js fetches all data and outputs data/draft-history.json

Limitations

  • No physical measurables (height, wingspan, athletic testing)
  • No combine/workout performance data
  • No team need or draft class depth context
  • International prospects not modeled (different stat context)
  • Class inferred from multi-season appearances — may be off for transfers or redshirts
  • MBB only — WBB draft dynamics differ significantly
  • PPG regularized to zero weight — scoring volume doesn't differentiate drafted vs. undrafted in training data
  • Eligibility gate: players with PPG < 5, MPG < 15, or G < 10 receive no draft grade (shown as —). These players fall outside the training distribution and predictions would be unreliable extrapolations
